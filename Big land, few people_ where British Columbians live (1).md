## Why it matters

Housing, transit, health care, schools and jobs all look different in a region with hundreds of people per square kilometre than in one with fewer than one. Maps that size regions by land can make B.C. look evenly settled. Maps that size them by people show where the pressure is.

## About the data

- **Population:** Statistics Canada, 2021 Census of Population (5,000,879 people). Census divisions are B.C.’s regional districts, plus Northern Rockies and Stikine.
- **Outline and land area:** province outline from the Statistics Canada boundary file; division land areas are 2021 Census land-area figures (water excluded), which sum to the provincial land area of about 920,700 km².
- **Blocks:** each map has 1,000 blocks, so one block is 0.1% of the total. Block counts are rounded and fitted to the B.C. outline, and every division gets at least one block, so they can differ slightly from exact shares (for example, Metro Vancouver has 502 population blocks against an exact share of about 528). The table gives exact figures.
- **Land map:** division borders are approximate (placed from each division’s centre and land area), so treat shapes as illustrative rather than survey-accurate.
- **Population map:** a density-equalizing cartogram snapped to a block grid. It stays close to B.C.’s outline, but Metro Vancouver is so large that the rest of the province is heavily compressed.

### How to read this kind of map

These are **gridded cartograms**, sometimes called block maps or tile grid maps. Instead of drawing each region at its real size, the whole province is split into equal blocks, and each region receives the share of blocks it deserves.

#### Equal blocks

Every block is the same size and stands for the same amount: 0.1% of B.C.’s land on the left map, and 0.1% of its people (about 5,000) on the right. A region’s size is simply how many blocks it has.

#### Why not a normal map?

A regular map shows land, so huge, thinly settled regions look important and small, crowded ones look minor. Equal blocks let every person count the same, so you can see where people actually live.

#### How to compare

Find a region on both maps. If it grows on the right, it holds a bigger share of people than of land. If it shrinks, the opposite. Metro Vancouver goes from about 3 blocks to about 500.

#### What it can’t show

Distances and exact shapes are not to scale, and a block is not a town or a place. The population map is stretched to stay close to B.C.’s outline, so read it for proportions, not directions.
